## 1. Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) establishes the requirements and procedures for the safe, compliant, and auditable shipment of temperature-sensitive biological specimens and investigational medicinal products (IMPs) requiring cold-chain management. This SOP ensures product integrity, regulatory compliance, and patient safety throughout the shipment lifecycle.

## 2. Scope

This SOP applies to all personnel involved in the preparation, packaging, documentation, shipment, and receipt of temperature-sensitive clinical materials including:

- Biological specimens requiring refrigerated (2°C to 8°C) or frozen (-20°C or below) conditions
- Investigational medicinal products (IMPs)
- Reagents and kits requiring temperature control
- Any materials designated as cold-chain sensitive by the sponsor or regulatory requirements

## 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Cold Chain** | An uninterrupted temperature-controlled logistics chain from origin to destination |
| **Temperature Excursion** | Any deviation from the specified temperature range during storage or transit |
| **Data Logger** | Electronic device that records temperature at predefined intervals during shipment |
| **Secondary Packaging** | Insulated container that maintains temperature during transit |
| **Calibration Certificate** | Document verifying accuracy of temperature monitoring equipment |
| **Chain of Custody** | Documentation tracking possession and handling of shipped materials |

## 4. Responsibilities

### 4.1 Clinical Operations Team
- Initiate shipment requests and ensure all documentation is complete
- Coordinate with packaging team for appropriate secondary packaging
- Verify temperature logger deployment and activation

### 4.2 Packaging Team
- Select and prepare appropriate insulated shipping containers
- Ensure proper placement of temperature conditioning materials (gel packs, dry ice, etc.)
- Verify packaging integrity before handoff to carrier

### 4.3 Vendor/Carrier
- Maintain temperature conditions during transit
- Provide calibrated temperature loggers with valid calibration certificates
- Ensure timely delivery within specified timeframes

### 4.4 Receiving Site
- Inspect shipment upon arrival
- Download and review temperature data
- Report any temperature excursions immediately

### 4.5 Quality Assurance
- Review and approve temperature excursion reports
- Maintain audit trails and documentation
- Conduct periodic SOP compliance audits

## 5. Equipment and Materials

### 5.1 Temperature Monitoring Devices
- Electronic data loggers must be used for all cold-chain shipments
- Loggers must have valid calibration certificates from the vendor
- Calibration must be current (within 12 months of shipment date)
- Logger placement must be documented in packing records

### 5.2 Secondary Packaging
- Insulated shipping containers appropriate for the temperature range and transit duration
- Phase change materials (PCM) or gel packs conditioned to appropriate temperatures
- Sufficient coolant to maintain temperature for minimum 72 hours beyond expected transit time
- Outer shipping carton with appropriate hazard labels if applicable

### 5.3 Temperature Ranges

| Product Type | Required Temperature Range |
|--------------|---------------------------|
| Refrigerated Products | 2°C to 8°C |
| Frozen Products | -20°C or below |
| Deep Frozen Products | -70°C or below |
| Controlled Room Temperature | 15°C to 25°C |

## 6. Procedure

### 6.1 Pre-Shipment Preparation

1. **Shipment Request Initiation**
   - Complete Shipment Request Form with product details, temperature requirements, and destination
   - Verify recipient site readiness and availability
   - Confirm shipping address and contact information

2. **Product Preparation**
   - Verify product identity and quantity against shipping manifest
   - Inspect product for any visible damage or compromise
   - Document product batch/lot numbers and expiry dates

3. **Temperature Logger Setup**
   - Obtain data logger from vendor with valid calibration certificate
   - Verify calibration certificate is current and on file
   - Activate logger according to manufacturer instructions
   - Set logging interval (recommended: every 15 minutes)
   - Document logger serial number in shipment records

### 6.2 Packaging Process

1. **Container Preparation**
   - Select insulated container appropriate for product volume and transit duration
   - Inspect container for damage or compromised insulation
   - Pre-condition container if required by manufacturer

2. **Coolant Preparation**
   - Condition gel packs/PCM to appropriate temperature (typically 24-48 hours)
   - Verify coolant temperature before use
   - Arrange coolant around product to ensure even temperature distribution

3. **Packing Sequence**
   - Place temperature buffer material at bottom of container
   - Position product in center of container (avoid direct contact with coolant)
   - Place activated temperature logger adjacent to product
   - Surround product with conditioned coolant on all sides
   - Place additional buffer material on top
   - Seal inner container securely

4. **Final Packaging**
   - Place sealed inner container in outer shipping carton
   - Add appropriate absorbent material for leak containment
   - Apply temperature-sensitive labels and orientation arrows
   - Seal outer carton with tamper-evident tape

### 6.3 Documentation

The following documents must accompany each shipment:

1. **Shipping Manifest**
   - Product name, description, and quantity
   - Batch/lot numbers
   - Expiry dates
   - Storage temperature requirements

2. **Temperature Monitoring Documentation**
   - Data logger serial number
   - Calibration certificate copy
   - Logger activation time and date

3. **Regulatory Documents**
   - Shipper's Declaration for Dangerous Goods (if applicable)
   - Import/export permits (if applicable)
   - Clinical trial authorization references

4. **Chain of Custody Form**
   - Origin and destination details
   - Handover signatures
   - Date and time of each transfer

### 6.4 Carrier Handoff

1. Verify carrier is approved for cold-chain shipments
2. Confirm estimated delivery date and time
3. Provide carrier with handling instructions and emergency contacts
4. Obtain proof of pickup with date/time stamp
5. Initiate shipment tracking in logistics system

### 6.5 In-Transit Monitoring

1. Monitor shipment status via carrier tracking system
2. Maintain contact with carrier for any delays
3. Document any known transit disruptions
4. Alert receiving site of any anticipated delays

### 6.6 Receipt and Verification

1. **Upon Delivery**
   - Inspect outer packaging for damage or tampering
   - Verify shipment identity against expected delivery
   - Document receipt date and time
   - Sign chain of custody form

2. **Temperature Data Review**
   - Download temperature data from logger immediately
   - Review temperature profile for entire transit period
   - Identify any temperature excursions
   - Generate temperature report and file with shipment records

3. **Product Inspection**
   - Inspect product for visible damage
   - Verify product temperature upon receipt
   - Document condition in receipt log

### 6.7 Temperature Excursion Management

1. **Excursion Identification**
   - Any reading outside specified temperature range constitutes an excursion
   - Document excursion details: duration, magnitude, product affected

2. **Excursion Reporting**
   - Notify Clinical Operations and Quality Assurance within 24 hours
   - Complete Temperature Excursion Report Form
   - Include temperature data printout and shipment documentation

3. **Excursion Assessment**
   - Quality Assurance reviews excursion with sponsor/stability data
   - Determine product disposition (use, quarantine, or destroy)
   - Document decision and rationale

4. **Corrective Actions**
   - Investigate root cause of excursion
   - Implement corrective and preventive actions (CAPA)
   - Update procedures if necessary

## 7. Record Keeping

All cold-chain shipment records must be retained for minimum periods as follows:

| Record Type | Retention Period |
|-------------|------------------|
| Shipping manifests | 15 years post-study completion |
| Temperature data logs | 15 years post-study completion |
| Calibration certificates | 5 years after expiry |
| Excursion reports | 15 years post-study completion |
| Chain of custody forms | 15 years post-study completion |

Records may be maintained in electronic or paper format and must be:
- Securely stored with access controls
- Readily retrievable for audit or inspection
- Backed up regularly if electronic

## 8. Training Requirements

All personnel involved in cold-chain shipments must complete:

1. Initial training on this SOP before performing any cold-chain activities
2. Annual refresher training
3. Documentation of training completion in personnel training files
4. Competency assessment for packaging operations

## 9. Quality Control and Audit

1. **Periodic Review**
   - This SOP shall be reviewed annually
   - Updates made as needed based on regulatory changes or process improvements

2. **Audit Trail**
   - All cold-chain shipments are subject to internal quality audit
   - External regulatory inspections may review cold-chain documentation
   - Non-compliance findings must be addressed through CAPA process

3. **Vendor Qualification**
   - Cold-chain carriers must be qualified before use
   - Vendor performance monitored through key performance indicators (KPIs)
   - Annual vendor review required
