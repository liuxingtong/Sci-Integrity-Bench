# Air Quality and Respiratory Health Policy Brief

## Key Findings from Daily Panel Analysis (2020–2022)

### Evidence Summary

A three-year daily panel analysis (N = 1,096 days) linking PM2.5 concentrations with respiratory clinic visits, heating degree days, influenza activity, and school holiday indicators reveals:

1. **PM2.5 is a significant driver of respiratory clinic visits** (β = 0.45 visits per μg/m³, p < 0.001), independent of seasonal confounders.

2. **WHO guideline exceedances are frequent**: PM2.5 exceeded 35 μg/m³ on 247 days (22.5% of the study period), with significantly higher respiratory visits on exceedance days.

3. **Winter is the highest-risk period**: PM2.5 peaks in winter due to heating combustion, coinciding with peak influenza activity and cold-induced respiratory stress.

4. **Lagged effects persist 3–4 days**: PM2.5 effects on respiratory visits remain statistically significant for up to 4 days after exposure.

5. **School holidays reduce visits by ~4 visits/day**, reflecting reduced population mixing and transmission.

### Model Performance
- R² = 0.48 (model explains 48% of daily visit variance)
- All four predictors (PM2.5, heating degree days, flu index, school holiday) are statistically significant (p < 0.001)

---

## Policy Recommendations

### Immediate (0–6 months)
- Implement real-time PM2.5 alert system for vulnerable populations
- Issue health advisories on days when PM2.5 > 35 μg/m³
- Coordinate flu vaccination campaigns with air quality monitoring

### Short-Term (6–24 months)
- Adopt WHO 2021 PM2.5 annual guideline (5 μg/m³)
- Establish tiered action plans at PM2.5 thresholds of 25, 35, and 50 μg/m³
- Implement air quality-contingent outdoor activity restrictions for schools

### Long-Term (2–10 years)
- Decarbonize heating sector (electrification, district heating, renewables)
- Establish integrated environmental health surveillance linking air quality and health records
- Quantify and communicate economic burden of PM2.5-attributable respiratory visits
