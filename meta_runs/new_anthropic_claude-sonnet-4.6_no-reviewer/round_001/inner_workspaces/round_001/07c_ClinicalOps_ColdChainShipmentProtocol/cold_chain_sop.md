# Cold-Chain Shipment Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

**Document ID:** SOP-CC-001  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Effective Date:** 2025-01-01  
**Prepared By:** Clinical Operations (ops@clinic.org)  
**Review Cycle:** Annual  

---

## 1. Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) establishes the requirements and responsibilities for the cold-chain shipment of biologics along the new biologics route. It ensures that temperature-sensitive products are transported under controlled conditions to maintain product integrity, patient safety, and regulatory compliance.

---

## 2. Scope

This SOP applies to all personnel involved in the preparation, shipment, monitoring, and receipt of cold-chain biologics, including:
- Clinical Operations team
- Packaging team
- Third-party logistics (3PL) vendors
- Receiving site personnel

---

## 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Cold Chain** | A temperature-controlled supply chain for products requiring refrigeration or freezing |
| **Biologics** | Medicinal products derived from biological sources requiring strict temperature control |
| **Data Logger** | An electronic device installed in transport vehicles to continuously record temperature |
| **Calibration** | The process of verifying that a measurement instrument (e.g., data logger) meets defined accuracy standards |
| **Secondary Packaging** | Outer packaging that contains the primary (immediate) packaging of the product |
| **Excursion** | Any deviation from the defined temperature range during transport |

---

## 4. Responsibilities

### 4.1 Clinical Operations (ops@clinic.org)
- Develop, maintain, and update this SOP
- Coordinate with vendors and packaging team
- Investigate and document temperature excursions
- Ensure regulatory compliance

### 4.2 Logistics Vendor
- Provide and maintain calibrated temperature data loggers in all transport vehicles
- Supply calibration certificates for all data loggers prior to each shipment
- Ensure vehicles meet required temperature specifications
- Provide shipment documentation and logger download reports upon delivery

### 4.3 Packaging Team
- Define and implement secondary packaging specifications for biologics
- Validate secondary packaging for thermal performance
- Provide packaging instructions and materials to shipping personnel
- Follow up on secondary packaging updates as required

### 4.4 Receiving Site Personnel
- Inspect shipments upon arrival
- Verify temperature logger data
- Document and report any excursions immediately
- Accept or reject shipments per defined criteria

---

## 5. Equipment and Materials

### 5.1 Temperature Monitoring
- **Data Loggers:** Calibrated electronic temperature loggers installed in all transport trucks
  - Calibration details and certificates to be obtained from the logistics vendor prior to each shipment
  - Calibration must be traceable to national/international standards (e.g., NIST)
  - Calibration interval: as specified by vendor, not to exceed 12 months
- **Backup Monitoring:** Chemical temperature indicators (e.g., time-temperature indicators) placed inside secondary packaging

### 5.2 Packaging Materials
- Primary packaging: as specified by product manufacturer
- Secondary packaging: to be defined and validated by the packaging team
  - Must maintain required temperature range for the duration of the longest anticipated transit time plus a 20% buffer
  - Insulated containers with appropriate coolant (e.g., gel packs, dry ice) as specified

---

## 6. Temperature Requirements

| Product Category | Required Temperature Range | Maximum Excursion Duration |
|-----------------|---------------------------|---------------------------|
| Refrigerated Biologics | +2°C to +8°C | ≤ 30 minutes cumulative |
| Frozen Biologics | ≤ -20°C | ≤ 15 minutes cumulative |
| Ultra-Cold Biologics | ≤ -60°C | ≤ 10 minutes cumulative |

*Note: Specific temperature requirements for each product must be confirmed against the product's Summary of Product Characteristics (SmPC) or Investigator's Brochure.*

---

## 7. Procedure

### 7.1 Pre-Shipment Preparation

**Step 1 — Obtain Calibration Documentation**
1. Contact the logistics vendor to obtain current calibration certificates for all data loggers to be used in the shipment.
2. Verify that calibration is current (within the defined calibration interval).
3. Record calibration certificate numbers in the Shipment Log (Appendix A).
4. If calibration certificates are unavailable or expired, do not proceed with shipment until resolved.

**Step 2 — Prepare Secondary Packaging**
1. Coordinate with the packaging team to obtain approved secondary packaging materials and instructions.
2. Condition coolant materials (gel packs, dry ice) per packaging team specifications.
3. Pack product in primary packaging inside validated secondary packaging.
4. Include a chemical temperature indicator inside each secondary package.
5. Seal and label secondary packages per applicable regulations.

**Step 3 — Vehicle Inspection**
1. Verify that the transport vehicle's refrigeration unit is functioning correctly.
2. Confirm that the data logger is active and recording.
3. Pre-condition the vehicle interior to the required temperature range before loading.
4. Document vehicle ID, logger ID, and pre-shipment temperature in the Shipment Log.

**Step 4 — Loading**
1. Load product only after vehicle interior has reached the required temperature range.
2. Minimize door-open time during loading.
3. Record loading start and end times in the Shipment Log.

### 7.2 In-Transit Monitoring

1. The data logger records temperature continuously throughout transit.
2. The logistics vendor is responsible for monitoring real-time temperature alerts (if remote monitoring is available).
3. In the event of a temperature alarm:
   a. The driver must contact Clinical Operations immediately.
   b. Clinical Operations will assess the situation and provide disposition instructions.
   c. All communications must be documented.

### 7.3 Receipt and Acceptance

**Step 1 — Arrival Inspection**
1. Receiving personnel inspect the shipment for physical damage.
2. Verify that the chemical temperature indicator shows no excursion.
3. Download and review data logger records.

**Step 2 — Data Logger Review**
1. Confirm that temperature remained within the required range throughout transit.
2. If an excursion is detected:
   a. Quarantine the affected product immediately.
   b. Notify Clinical Operations within 2 hours.
   c. Complete the Temperature Excursion Report (Appendix B).
   d. Do not use product until disposition is determined by Clinical Operations.

**Step 3 — Documentation**
1. Record receipt date, time, and condition in the Shipment Log.
2. Attach data logger download report to the Shipment Log.
3. File all documentation per the site's document management system.

### 7.4 Temperature Excursion Management

1. Clinical Operations will investigate all excursions within 5 business days.
2. Investigation will include:
   - Review of logger data and calibration records
   - Assessment of product impact (in consultation with the manufacturer or QA)
   - Root cause analysis
   - Corrective and Preventive Actions (CAPA)
3. Product disposition (use, quarantine, or destruction) will be documented and approved by Clinical Operations and QA.
4. Recurring excursions will trigger a vendor audit.

## 9. Training

- All personnel involved in cold-chain shipment activities must complete training on this SOP before performing any related tasks.
- Training must be documented in the site's training management system.
- Retraining is required upon SOP revision or following a significant excursion event.
