# Standard Operating Procedure: Copper Nanoparticle Synthesis (Pilot Scale)

## 1.0 Purpose
This SOP describes the procedure for synthesizing copper nanoparticles via thermal decomposition method at pilot scale (1-10 L batch size).

## 2.0 Scope
This procedure applies to the synthesis of copper nanoparticles for research and development purposes. The method produces nanoparticles with controlled size and morphology through thermal decomposition of a copper precursor in a non-polar solvent with surfactant stabilization.

## 3.0 Safety Precautions

### 3.1 Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)
- Lab coat
- Safety goggles
- Heat-resistant gloves
- Closed-toe shoes
- Face shield (when handling hot oil bath)

### 3.2 Chemical Hazards
- Precursor A: Copper(II) acetylacetonate or similar copper salt (irritant)
- Surfactant: Oleylamine or similar (corrosive, irritant)
- Solvent: Octadecene or similar high-boiling solvent (flammable)
- All chemicals should be handled in fume hood

### 3.3 Emergency Procedures
- Know location of: eyewash station, safety shower, fire extinguisher
- In case of fire: use CO₂ fire extinguisher, do not use water
- Chemical spill: contain with absorbent material, dispose as hazardous waste

## 4.0 Equipment and Materials

### 4.1 Equipment
- 3-neck round-bottom flask (appropriate size for batch)
- Heating mantle with temperature controller
- Thermometer/thermocouple
- Condenser (reflux)
- Magnetic stirrer with heating plate
- Oil bath (silicone oil)
- Separatory funnel
- Centrifuge
- Vacuum oven
- Schlenk line or nitrogen/vacuum manifold (for inert atmosphere)

### 4.2 Materials
- Precursor A: Copper(II) acetylacetonate (Cu(acac)₂) or copper oleate
- Surfactant: Oleylamine (technical grade, 70%)
- Solvent: 1-Octadecene (ODE, technical grade, 90%)
- Co-solvent: Toluene or hexane (for washing)
- Anti-solvent: Ethanol or methanol (for precipitation)
- Nitrogen gas (high purity, 99.99%)

## 5.0 Procedure

### 5.1 Preparation
1. Clean all glassware with aqua regia (3:1 HCl:HNO₃) followed by rinsing with deionized water and acetone. Dry in oven at 120°C for 2 hours.
2. Set up 3-neck flask with condenser, thermometer, and septum.
3. Connect to Schlenk line or nitrogen/vacuum manifold.
4. Prepare oil bath and heat to 110°C ± 5°C.

### 5.2 Synthesis
1. Charge the flask with solvent (1-Octadecene) at 0.1 M concentration relative to precursor.
2. Purge the system with nitrogen for 30 minutes while stirring at 300 rpm.
3. Heat the solution to 110°C under nitrogen flow.
4. Dissolve Precursor A (copper salt) in minimal amount of solvent at 60°C.
5. Add Precursor A solution dropwise (1 drop per second) to the hot solvent using a syringe pump or dropping funnel.
6. Immediately add Surfactant (oleylamine) at 2:1 molar ratio (surfactant:precursor).
7. Continue stirring at 300 rpm under nitrogen atmosphere.
8. Maintain temperature at 110°C for 12-16 hours (overnight).

### 5.3 Monitoring
- Observe color change from initial blue-green to dark brown/black.
- Monitor temperature continuously (±2°C tolerance).
- Check for precipitation or aggregation.

### 5.4 Quenching and Workup
1. After reaction time, remove flask from oil bath and allow to cool to 60°C.
2. Add anti-solvent (ethanol) at 3:1 volume ratio (anti-solvent:reaction mixture).
3. Centrifuge at 8000 rpm for 10 minutes.
4. Decant supernatant.
5. Redisperse nanoparticles in toluene or hexane.
6. Repeat precipitation/centrifugation cycle 3 times.
7. Final product: disperse in non-polar solvent or dry as powder.

### 5.5 Drying (Optional)
1. Transfer washed nanoparticles to glass vial.
2. Place in vacuum oven at 60°C for 6 hours.
3. Store under nitrogen or argon atmosphere.

## 6.0 Quality Control

### 6.1 Visual Inspection
- Color: Dark brown to black suspension
- Homogeneity: No visible aggregates or sedimentation
- Clarity: Opaque but uniform

### 6.2 Characterization (Recommended)
- UV-Vis spectroscopy: Should show surface plasmon resonance at ~570-600 nm
- TEM: Determine particle size (target: 5-20 nm) and morphology
- XRD: Confirm crystalline copper phase
- FTIR: Verify surfactant coating

## 7.0 Troubleshooting

| Problem | Possible Cause | Solution |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| No color change | Temperature too low | Increase to 120°C, check thermometer calibration |
| Aggregation | Insufficient surfactant | Increase surfactant ratio to 3:1 |
| Precipitation during reaction | Solvent decomposition | Use fresh solvent, check purity |
| Blue color persists | Incomplete reduction | Extend reaction time to 24 hours |
| Broad size distribution | Fast precursor addition | Slow dropwise addition (1 drop/2 seconds) |

## 8.0 Scale-up Considerations

### 8.1 Batch Size Adjustment
- Maintain constant concentration (0.1 M)
- Increase stirring rate proportionally to volume
- Ensure adequate heat transfer (larger surface area vessels)
- Consider mechanical stirring for >5 L batches

### 8.2 Safety at Scale
- Use jacketed reactor with temperature control
- Install pressure relief valve
- Implement emergency cooling system
- Conduct hazard and operability study (HAZOP)
