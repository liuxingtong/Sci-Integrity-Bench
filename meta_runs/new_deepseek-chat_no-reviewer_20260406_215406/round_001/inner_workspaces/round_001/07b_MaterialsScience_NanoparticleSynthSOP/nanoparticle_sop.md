# Standard Operating Procedure: Copper Nanoparticle Synthesis (Pilot Scale)

## 1. Purpose
This SOP describes the procedure for synthesizing copper nanoparticles (NanoCu) at pilot scale for reproducible production.

## 2. Scope
This procedure applies to the synthesis of copper nanoparticles using thermal decomposition method in organic phase.

## 3. Safety Precautions
- Wear appropriate PPE: lab coat, safety glasses, heat-resistant gloves
- Work in fume hood when handling chemicals
- Be aware of hot oil bath hazards (110°C)
- Know location of fire extinguisher and emergency shower

## 4. Materials and Equipment
### 4.1 Chemicals
- Precursor A: Copper(II) acetylacetonate (Cu(acac)₂) or similar copper precursor
- Surfactant: Oleylamine (OAm) or Oleic acid (OA)
- Solvent: Octadecene (ODE) or similar high-boiling solvent
- Quenching solvent: Ethanol or methanol

### 4.2 Equipment
- Three-neck round-bottom flask (500 mL - 1 L for pilot scale)
- Reflux condenser
- Thermometer or temperature probe
- Magnetic stirrer with heating plate
- Oil bath setup
- Syringe pump or peristaltic pump for dropwise addition
- Separation funnel
- Centrifuge (for pilot scale workup)
- Vacuum oven

## 5. Procedure

### 5.1 Preparation
1. Clean all glassware thoroughly and dry in oven.
2. Set up three-neck flask with reflux condenser and thermometer.
3. Prepare oil bath and heat to 110°C ± 5°C.
4. Weigh required quantities:
   - Precursor A: XX g (calculate based on desired nanoparticle yield)
   - Surfactant: YY mL (typically 5-10 mL per 100 mg precursor)
   - Solvent: ZZ mL (enough to achieve 0.1-0.2 M precursor concentration)

### 5.2 Synthesis
1. Add solvent to the three-neck flask.
2. Heat solvent to 110°C under inert atmosphere (N₂ or Ar flow).
3. Dissolve Precursor A in minimal amount of solvent or surfactant.
4. Using syringe pump, add Precursor A solution dropwise to the hot solvent over 30-60 minutes.
5. Immediately add surfactant to the reaction mixture.
6. Maintain temperature at 110°C and stir continuously (500-1000 rpm).
7. Continue reaction for 12-16 hours (overnight).

### 5.3 Monitoring
- Observe color change: initial blue-green (copper precursor) should transition to brown (copper nanoparticles formation).
- Monitor temperature stability (±2°C).
- Check for any precipitation or aggregation.

### 5.4 Quenching and Workup
1. After reaction time, remove flask from oil bath.
2. Allow to cool to room temperature (~30 minutes).
3. Add quenching solvent (ethanol/methanol) in 1:2 volume ratio to reaction mixture.
4. Transfer to centrifuge tubes and centrifuge at 8000-10000 rpm for 10 minutes.
5. Discard supernatant.
6. Redisperse nanoparticles in non-polar solvent (hexane, toluene).
7. Repeat centrifugation and washing 2-3 times.
8. Final product can be stored as dispersion or dried in vacuum oven at 40°C.

## 6. Quality Control
### 6.1 Visual Inspection
- Color: Dark brown dispersion, no visible aggregates
- Stability: No precipitation after 24 hours at room temperature

### 6.2 Characterization (Recommended)
- UV-Vis spectroscopy: Should show plasmon resonance ~570-600 nm
- TEM: Determine particle size and morphology
- XRD: Confirm crystalline copper phase

## 7. Troubleshooting
| Problem | Possible Cause | Solution |
|---------|---------------|----------|
| No color change | Temperature too low | Verify temperature calibration |
| Black precipitate | Overheating or oxidation | Check inert atmosphere, reduce temperature |
| Poor dispersion | Insufficient surfactant | Increase surfactant ratio |
| Broad size distribution | Fast precursor addition | Slow down dropwise addition rate |

## 8. Scale-up Notes
- For pilot scale (>100 mL reaction volume):
  - Ensure efficient mixing (consider mechanical stirring)
  - Monitor heat distribution in larger vessels
  - Adjust addition times proportionally to volume
  - Consider continuous flow setup for larger production
