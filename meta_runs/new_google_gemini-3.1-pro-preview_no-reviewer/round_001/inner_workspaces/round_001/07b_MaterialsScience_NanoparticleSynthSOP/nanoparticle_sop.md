# Standard Operating Procedure (SOP): Pilot-Scale Synthesis of Copper Nanoparticles (NanoCu)

## 1. Purpose
This document outlines the standard operating procedure for the pilot-scale synthesis of copper nanoparticles (NanoCu) based on bench-scale optimization. The process involves the thermal decomposition/reduction of a copper precursor in the presence of a surfactant.

## 2. Scope
This SOP applies to the pilot-scale production (e.g., 10L - 50L reactor volume) of NanoCu. It covers equipment preparation, reaction execution, quenching, and downstream workup.

## 3. Safety Precautions
*   **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):** Safety goggles, lab coat, thermal gloves (for handling hot equipment), and chemical-resistant gloves (Nitrile).
*   **Ventilation:** All operations involving volatile solvents must be conducted under a fume hood or with appropriate local exhaust ventilation.
*   **Thermal Hazards:** The reaction operates at 110°C. Avoid contact with the reactor jacket, transfer lines, and hot reaction mixtures.
*   **Inert Atmosphere:** Copper nanoparticles are highly susceptible to oxidation. The reaction and subsequent handling should be performed under an inert atmosphere (Nitrogen or Argon) where possible.

## 4. Equipment and Materials
### 4.1 Equipment
*   Jacketed glass reactor (10L - 50L) equipped with a bottom drain valve.
*   Overhead mechanical stirrer with a PTFE impeller.
*   Temperature Control Unit (TCU) capable of heating to at least 150°C and rapid cooling.
*   Addition funnels or dosing pumps for controlled reagent addition.
*   Inert gas (N2 or Ar) manifold and bubbler.
*   High-capacity centrifuge and compatible centrifuge bottles.
*   Vacuum drying oven.

### 4.2 Reagents
*   **Precursor A:** Copper(II) salt (e.g., Copper(II) acetylacetonate or Copper(II) oleate).
*   **Surfactant:** Capping agent/reducing assistant (e.g., Oleylamine).
*   **Solvent:** High-boiling point solvent (e.g., 1-Octadecene).
*   **Quenching/Resuspension Solvent:** Non-polar solvent (e.g., Toluene or Hexane).
*   **Anti-solvent (Washing):** Polar solvent (e.g., Ethanol or Acetone).

## 5. Procedure

### 5.1 Reactor Preparation and Heating
1.  Ensure the reactor is clean, dry, and fully assembled.
2.  Initiate an inert gas (N2 or Ar) purge through the reactor for at least 30 minutes to displace oxygen.
3.  Charge the reactor with the primary high-boiling **Solvent**.
4.  Set the overhead stirrer to a moderate speed (e.g., 200-300 RPM).
5.  Program the TCU to heat the reactor contents to **110°C**.

### 5.2 Reagent Addition
1.  Once the internal temperature stabilizes at 110°C, begin the addition of **Precursor A**.
2.  **CRITICAL STEP:** Add Precursor A **dropwise** (or at a controlled slow rate via dosing pump) to manage any exothermic behavior and control the initial nucleation rate.
3.  Following the complete addition of Precursor A, add the **Surfactant** to the reactor.

### 5.3 Reaction (Overnight Stirring)
1.  Maintain the reactor temperature at 110°C and continue stirring.
2.  Allow the reaction to proceed overnight (approximately 12-16 hours).
3.  **Visual Monitoring:** Monitor the color of the reaction mixture. The initial mixture will appear **blue-green** (characteristic of Cu(II) complexes). As the reduction to Cu(0) nanoparticles progresses, the color will transition to a deep **brown/burgundy**.

### 5.4 Quenching
1.  After the overnight reaction period, initiate the quenching process to arrest nanoparticle growth and prevent agglomeration.
2.  Program the TCU to rapidly cool the reactor to room temperature (20°C - 25°C).
3.  Once the temperature drops below 50°C, add a volume of **Quenching Solvent** (e.g., Toluene) equivalent to approximately 50% of the initial reaction volume to dilute the mixture and further stabilize the particles.

### 5.5 Workup and Purification
1.  Discharge the cooled reaction mixture from the reactor via the bottom drain valve into appropriate transfer vessels.
2.  Transfer the mixture into centrifuge bottles.
3.  Add an equal volume of **Anti-solvent** (e.g., Ethanol) to the centrifuge bottles to induce precipitation of the NanoCu.
4.  Centrifuge the mixture at high speed (e.g., 8000 RPM for 10-15 minutes).
5.  Carefully decant and discard the clear supernatant.
6.  **Washing Cycle:** Resuspend the dark brown pellet in a minimal amount of non-polar solvent (Hexane/Toluene), sonicate briefly if necessary to ensure dispersion, and then precipitate again by adding Anti-solvent (Ethanol). Centrifuge and discard the supernatant.
7.  Repeat the washing cycle 2 to 3 times to remove excess unreacted precursor, surfactant, and high-boiling solvent.

### 5.6 Drying and Storage
1.  Transfer the final washed NanoCu paste to a drying dish.
2.  Dry the material in a vacuum oven at room temperature or slightly elevated temperature (max 40°C) overnight to remove residual volatile solvents.
3.  Store the resulting dry NanoCu powder in an airtight container, ideally backfilled with inert gas, and keep in a cool, dark place to minimize oxidation.

## 6. Troubleshooting
*   **Incomplete Color Change:** If the mixture remains blue-green after overnight stirring, the reduction is incomplete. Check the temperature calibration of the TCU and ensure the correct ratio of precursor to surfactant/reducing agent was used.
*   **Agglomeration/Precipitation during Reaction:** If black precipitate forms rapidly during the reaction, the heating rate may have been too fast, or the precursor addition was not sufficiently controlled (dropwise). Ensure rigorous stirring and controlled addition rates.
