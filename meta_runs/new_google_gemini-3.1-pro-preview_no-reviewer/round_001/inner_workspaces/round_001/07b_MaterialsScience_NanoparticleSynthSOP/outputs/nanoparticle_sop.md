# Standard Operating Procedure: Pilot-Scale Synthesis of Copper Nanoparticles (NanoCu)

## 1. Objective
To synthesize copper nanoparticles (NanoCu) at a pilot scale using a thermal decomposition/reduction method, translated from bench-scale laboratory notes.

## 2. Safety and PPE
- Safety goggles, lab coat, and nitrile gloves.
- Perform all operations in a well-ventilated fume hood or pilot plant facility with appropriate exhaust.
- Handle hot oil baths and jacketed reactors with thermal protective gloves.
- Copper precursors and organic surfactants can be toxic and irritants; avoid skin contact and inhalation.

## 3. Materials and Equipment
- **Precursor A:** Copper precursor (e.g., Copper(II) acetate or Copper(II) acetylacetonate). *Refer to specific bottle for concentration/purity.*
- **Surfactant:** (e.g., Oleylamine, which acts as both surfactant and mild reducing agent).
- **Quenching/Dilution Solvent:** Non-polar solvent (e.g., Toluene or Hexane).
- **Washing/Precipitation Solvent:** Polar anti-solvent (e.g., Ethanol or Acetone).
- Pilot-scale jacketed reactor (or large round-bottom flask) with mechanical stirrer.
- Heating system (oil bath or reactor jacket temperature controller).
- Dropping funnel or automated dosing pump.
- High-capacity centrifuge.
- Inert gas supply (Nitrogen or Argon).

## 4. Procedure

### 4.1. Preparation and Heating
1. Ensure the reactor is clean, dry, and thoroughly purged with inert gas (N2 or Ar) for at least 30 minutes to displace oxygen and prevent oxidation of the copper nanoparticles.
2. Maintain a continuous, gentle flow of inert gas throughout the entire synthesis.
3. Heat the oil bath (or reactor jacket) to a setpoint of **110 °C**.

### 4.2. Addition of Reagents
1. Load the required volume of Precursor A into the dropping funnel or dosing system.
2. Once the reactor reaches 110 °C, begin adding Precursor A **dropwise** into the reactor. Ensure the addition rate is slow enough to maintain the reactor temperature without significant drops.
3. Following the complete addition of Precursor A, add the surfactant to the reaction mixture.

### 4.3. Reaction Phase
1. Ensure the mechanical stirrer is operating at a sufficient speed to provide vigorous mixing without excessive splashing.
2. Maintain the reaction temperature at 110 °C.
3. Stir the mixture continuously **overnight** (approximately 14-16 hours).
4. **Visual Monitoring:** Monitor the color of the reaction mixture. The initial mixture will be **blue-green**. As the reaction progresses and Cu(II) is reduced to Cu(0) nanoparticles, the color must transition to a deep **brown**. Do not proceed to the quench phase until this brown color is uniformly achieved.

### 4.4. Quench and Workup
*(Note: This section expands upon the incomplete bench notes to provide a standard nanoparticle isolation protocol)*
1. **Quench:** Turn off the heating system. Remove the heat source (e.g., lower the oil bath) and allow the reaction mixture to cool. 
2. Once the temperature drops below 60 °C, add a volume of non-polar solvent (e.g., toluene) equal to the reaction volume to dilute the mixture, arrest further particle growth, and prevent the surfactant from solidifying.
3. **Precipitation:** Transfer the mixture to centrifuge bottles. Add an excess (typically 3x volume) of a polar anti-solvent (e.g., ethanol) to precipitate the copper nanoparticles.
4. **Separation:** Centrifuge the mixture at high speed (e.g., 8000 rpm for 10-15 minutes). The NanoCu will form a brown pellet at the bottom. Carefully decant and discard the clear supernatant.
5. **Washing:** Resuspend the brown pellet in a minimal amount of non-polar solvent (hexane/toluene) using sonication if necessary. Add ethanol to precipitate the particles again, and centrifuge. Repeat this washing cycle 2 to 3 times to remove all unreacted precursor and excess surfactant.
6. **Drying:** Dry the final NanoCu powder under vacuum at room temperature. **Crucial:** Store the dried brown powder immediately in an inert atmosphere (glovebox or sealed under Argon) to prevent oxidation to copper oxide.

## 5. Quality Control
- **Visual:** The final product must be a brown powder or a stable brown dispersion in non-polar solvents.
- **Analytical:** Submit samples for UV-Vis spectroscopy (check for characteristic surface plasmon resonance), X-ray Diffraction (XRD) to confirm the Cu(0) face-centered cubic crystal structure, and Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM) for size distribution analysis.
